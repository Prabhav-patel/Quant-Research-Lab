Evidence standard

  • Show the source and observation window.
  • Separate model output from realised performance.
  • Compare against an independent formula or simple baseline.
  • State failed tests and unverified components plainly.

How to read the results

Research is not a backtest screenshot

Market-data studies need explicit sequencing, missing-event checks, non-overlapping prediction horizons, fees, spread, slippage and a genuinely held-out period. A local paper-trading simulator is a useful systems test; it cannot establish execution quality.

The next HFT experiment would measure signal lift by horizon and day, then test whether it survives a conservative fill model. That study is proposed, not completed.

02 · Portfolio and risk

May 2025 · JPM / MS

Does a two-bank portfolio diversify?

Method: adjusted JPM and MS prices, an illustrative 50/50 daily-rebalanced return series, Sharpe, Sortino, drawdown and correlation over roughly six years.

Correlation 0.74 · portfolio volatility 25.01%

The pair offers limited sector diversification. The reported 28.50% historical CAGR and 0.98 Sharpe are sample outcomes, not forecasts; assumptions include a 3.68% mean short-rate proxy and zero trading costs.

Mar 2025 · VaR / ES / backtesting

Does 99% VaR actually cover the losses?

Method: an equal-weight JPM/MS/AAPL illustrative portfolio; historical, Gaussian and Monte Carlo VaR/ES; rolling 252-day historical VaR evaluated only on subsequent observations.

23 exceptions / 1,254 days · Kupiec p = 0.008

About 12.5 breaches were expected; the full-period calibration test rejects at 5%. The last-250-day traffic light is green (0 breaches), but that is a different window and does not override the rejection. A static historical window can miss changing volatility.

03 · Derivatives and rates

Jan 2026 · NIFTY volatility

What changes when volatility clusters?

Method: constant, 21-day rolling and fitted GARCH(1,1) volatility scenarios drive matched Monte Carlo option payoffs; the constant-volatility run is cross-checked against Black–Scholes.

ATM: constant 506 · rolling 352 · GARCH 412

These are scenario prices in index points with an assumed 6.5% INR risk-free rate, not observed option premiums. GARCH is fitted to historical returns under the physical measure; using it in risk-neutral pricing is an educational approximation, not a calibrated tradable smile.

Jan 2025 · Vasicek / CIR

How do mean-reverting rates behave near zero?

Method: estimate Vasicek and CIR parameters from a four-year series of the US 13-week Treasury-bill yield proxy (^IRX), then compare simulated paths and model-implied curves.

Illustrative low-rate case: Vasicek 79.4% negative paths; CIR 0%

That low-rate experiment is synthetic. ^IRX is a quoted short-yield proxy, not the instantaneous short rate; estimated parameters should not be read as market-implied curve calibration.

Oct 2024 · path-dependent Monte Carlo

Can simulation be cross-checked?

Method: simulate AAPL-based GBM paths for European, arithmetic/geometric Asian and up-and-in/out barrier calls; compare known analytical prices and barrier parity.

European MC 39.275 vs BS 39.310 · Asian SE 0.1340 → 0.0045

The control variate reduces estimated standard error by about 30× in this run. AAPL spot/volatility inform the illustration; no observed option-chain quotes or market calibration are used.

May 2024 · pricing-method comparison

Do three European-call pricers agree?

Method: Black–Scholes formula, a convergent binomial tree and Monte Carlo simulation using the same illustrative AAPL inputs, strike and maturity.

Call: BS 39.309 · tree 39.304 · MC 39.249 ± 0.095

Agreement checks the implementation under shared assumptions; it does not show the theoretical price equals a live market quote. American early exercise is a separate lattice capability.

04 · Time series

Sep 2024 · NIFTY ARIMA

Does ARIMA improve a simple forecast?

Method: ADF checks on log prices and returns; a small ARIMA order grid selected on training data by AIC; residual diagnostics; 30-session held-out comparison with a random-walk forecast.

RMSE: ARIMA 966.2 vs random walk 960.5

ARIMA(1,1,1) does not beat the baseline on this holdout. Ljung–Box p = 0.871 means no residual autocorrelation was detected at lag 10; it does not prove forecast value or white noise.
